From policy to practice

Your procedures are written. Put them to work.

Procedures can be approved and published in an information security management system (ISMS) while everyday development drifts. Argus turns the requirements that can be automated into checks the team sees as it works.

A missing SBOM in a new project, or evidence older than your policy allows, becomes a failing pipeline check. The team can prioritise it in sprint planning, assign the fix and see the result on the next run, just like any other development issue.

Periodic attestation

“The SBOM was present when I checked.”

checked: on review day
recorded: by the reviewer

Continuous evidence

“The SBOM was present in every checked build.”

checked: on each pipeline run
recorded: result, time and commit

How Argus works

A rules engine that keeps the evidence.

01

Define the checks

Translate your requirements and procedures into rules. Start with supplied rule sets or define your own checks for documents, specified content and evidence freshness.

02

Run with development

Add Argus to your pipeline and choose when it runs. Pass/fail results make the checks part of the team’s normal workflow. Jira and GitLab connectors can add evidence from your development systems.

03

Capture the evidence

Capture each run’s results with the check time and Git commit when available. Keep reports locally or submit them to Command Central to build an evidence history as development proceeds.
